## Model choice

Gu's Secs. 14.1–14.2 give a general rate model for the RFC, here in "elution
with inert mobile phase" mode (index = 2): a rectangular pulse of duration
tau_imp is injected at tau = 0, after which the feed switches to pure carrier
(Eq. 14.12),

    Cf_i(tau)/C0_i = 1   for 0 < tau < tau_imp
                   = 0   otherwise

Both components follow a two-component Langmuir isotherm at rapid equilibrium
(Eq. 3.21), with film mass transfer and pore diffusion into spherical
particles and velocity-dependent bulk dispersion and film mass transfer.

That maps onto CADET as follows.

- `COLUMN_MODEL_1D` with `GEOMETRY='RADIAL_FLOW_CYLINDER_SHELL'` discretizes
  the bulk equation (Eqs. 14.7/14.8) in the physical radial coordinate X, so
  no substitution to an equivalent axial column is involved.
- `GENERAL_RATE_PARTICLE` resolves film and pore diffusion separately, as the
  paper's own particle equations do; a lumped rate approximation would not
  reproduce them.
- Velocity-dependent bulk dispersion, Db_i(X) ~ v(X) (Eq. 14.15), becomes
  `COL_DISPERSION_DEP='POWER_LAW'` with `EXPONENT=1`.
- Velocity-dependent film mass transfer, k_i(X) ~ v(X)^(1/3) (Eq. 14.16),
  becomes `FILM_DIFFUSION_DEP='POWER_LAW'` with `EXPONENT=1/3`.
- The dimensionless isotherm cp*_i = a_i*cp_i/(1+sum_j b_j*C0_j*cp_j) is
  `MULTI_COMPONENT_LANGMUIR` with `IS_KINETIC=0`, ka_i = b_i, kd_i = 1 and
  qmax_i = a_i/b_i; the C0_j cancel when the isotherm is written dimensionally.

## From Gu's dimensionless groups to CADET parameters

Gu's definitions (Ch. 3 and 14, in his notation):

    Pe_Li = v(X1)*(X1-X0)/Db_i,V=1        Bi_i = k_i*Rp/(eps_p*Dp_i)
    eta_i = eps_p*Dp_i*(X1-X0)/(Rp^2*v_char)
    tau   = v_char*t/(X1-X0),   v_char := 2*v(X1)*X1/(X1+X0)
    V0    = X0^2/(X1^2-X0^2)

v_char is the harmonic-mean transit velocity, chosen by Gu so that an
unretained tracer leaves the column at tau = 1.

A dimensionless model fixes no absolute scale, so the physical one can be
chosen freely: X1 = 0.05 m (outer radius, which is the inlet for inward flow),
Rp = 5e-5 m, v(X1) = 1e-4 m/s, eps_b = eps_p = 0.4 (given by the paper) and a
reference concentration of 1 mol/m^3. Inverting the definitions above then
gives

    X0 = X1*sqrt(V0/(1+V0));  L := X1-X0
    v_char = 2*v(X1)*X1/(X1+X0)
    Db_i|V=1 = v(X1)*L/PeL_i
    Dp_i     = eta_i*Rp^2*v_char/(eps_p*L)
    k_i|V=1  = Bi_i|V=1 * eta_i * Rp * v_char/L
    ka_i = b_i,  kd_i = 1,  qmax_i = a_i/b_i
    Q        = v(X1)*X1*2*pi*H*eps_b

The flow rate Q is picked so that CADET's velocity at the outer radius,
Q/(2*pi*H*eps_b*X1), comes out as v(X1).

One conversion is easy to get wrong. Both power-law dependencies are
evaluated at the local velocity everywhere in the column, not at a fixed
reference velocity, so the configured coefficients have to be divided by the
reference velocity (or its cube root) first:

    COL_DISPERSION[i] := Db_i|V=1 / v(X1)
        ->  Db_i(X) = COL_DISPERSION[i]*v(X) = Db_i|V=1 * v(X)/v(X1)
    FILM_DIFFUSION[i] := k_i|V=1 / v(X1)^(1/3)
        ->  k_i(X) = FILM_DIFFUSION[i]*v(X)^(1/3) = k_i|V=1 * (v(X)/v(X1))^(1/3)

This is part of the unit conversion, not an extra approximation: the
dimensionless groups are reproduced at the reference velocity and the
velocity scaling of Eqs. 14.15/14.16 holds everywhere else.

## Further notes

- The injection runs from tau = 0 to tau_imp = 0.5, i.e. physical time 0 to
  tau_imp*L/v_char, and the simulation continues to tau = 16, the x-axis range
  of the paper's own plot.
- `film_diffusion_velocity_dep=False` switches to Gu's "iave=2" approximation,
  which evaluates k_i once at V = 0.5,
  k_i|avg = k_i|V=1 * [(1-V0)/(0.5+V0)]^(1/6), instead of resolving Bi_i(V) in
  space. It is kept as a comparison option; the default is the
  position-dependent form.
- `col_dispersion_velocity_dep=False` likewise holds COL_DISPERSION at
  Db_i|V=1 everywhere. The paper offers no constant-dispersion variant, so
  this only shows what the dependency contributes.
